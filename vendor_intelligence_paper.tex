\begin{abstract}
Accounts-payable teams that reconcile purchase orders, goods receipts, and vendor invoices by hand or through static threshold heuristics routinely struggle with invoice overbilling, quantity mismatches, non-contractual unit rates, and unmonitored freight surcharges. Furthermore, enterprise audit workflows require robust batch ingestion, deduplication, and zero-downtime persistence to handle fluctuating commercial invoice loads. This paper presents the Vendor Intelligence and Performance System (VIPS)---an enterprise-grade automated invoice audit and vendor risk assessment platform. 

VIPS implements a strictly decoupled four-layer architecture comprising: (1) a high-performance FastAPI REST gateway strictly validated by Pydantic~v2 schemas; (2) an asynchronous batch ingestion engine powered by whole-file SHA-256 deduplication hashing, bulk purchase-order pre-fetching, 100-row transactional chunking, and row-level fault isolation logging; (3) a dual-model machine learning core featuring a 300-tree \texttt{RandomForestClassifier} trained on standardized invoice and purchase-order features alongside an Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) freight baseline regressor ($\hat{F} \approx 0.005015 \times \text{Invoice\$} + 5.008$), complemented by automated catalog PO matching and numeric sanity enforcement; and (4) an automated dual-database persistence layer (primary PostgreSQL with zero-downtime local SQLite failover) backed by a 17-query deterministic SQL analytics assistant and an interactive single-page Manifest Desk dashboard. 

Local benchmarking demonstrates sub-millisecond ML inference ($<2$\,ms), sub-15\,ms SQL query routing, and batch ingestion throughput of 350--500 rows per second. Feature importance analysis confirms that authorized purchase-order expenditure (29.21\%) and quantity (20.53\%) govern nearly half of every risk decision. The platform provides complete audit trail reproducibility, non-blocking ingestion for multi-thousand-row CSV submissions, and grounded analytics that maintain human reviewers at the center of final payment approvals.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================

In modern commercial operations, enterprise procurement departments manage thousands of business-to-business (B2B) transactions across complex supplier networks. Every incoming vendor invoice must undergo rigorous three-way matching---verifying that line-item quantities, unit prices, and supplemental freight surcharges strictly align with authorized Purchase Orders (POs) and warehouse receiving records before disbursement. Historically, accounts-payable (AP) teams have relied on manual sampling or static, threshold-based heuristics embedded within Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) databases. Both methodologies exhibit severe operational vulnerabilities when processing enterprise-scale transaction volumes.

Manual invoice reconciliation is labor-intensive, error-prone, and inconsistent. Reviewers applying subjective rules of thumb frequently arrive at divergent verdicts for identical billing patterns. Conversely, static rule engines operate under rigid tolerance bounds: thresholds tuned to detect obvious overbilling fail to identify subtle multi-variable anomalies across line items, while sensitive configurations trigger severe false-alarm fatigue. Furthermore, ancillary billing items---specifically logistics and freight handling fees---are rarely accompanied by objective contractual benchmarks and are routinely accepted on vendor trust, creating substantial unmonitored financial leakage.

Beyond single-invoice verification, enterprise accounts-payable pipelines face severe operational engineering hurdles. Vendor billing data arrives in high-volume CSV batch feeds. Existing systems frequently lack cryptographic deduplication (leading to redundant inference cycles and database clutter), abort multi-thousand-row uploads upon encountering an isolated malformed row, and suffer complete downtime during localized database network partitions.

To address these critical deficiencies, this paper presents the Vendor Intelligence and Performance System (VIPS). VIPS combines calibrated ensemble classification, linear freight cost baselining, automated catalog purchase-order verification, and an ultra-fast 17-query deterministic SQL analytics engine within an asynchronous, resilient microservice architecture. By coupling probabilistic anomaly triage with deterministic database analytics and an automatic PostgreSQL-to-SQLite failover mechanism, VIPS empowers human reviewers with transparent, auditable evidence while eliminating manual auditing friction.

\section{Problem Statement and Research Gap}
% =========================================================

Commercial accounts-payable solutions and prior academic frameworks typically cluster around three opposing architectural extremes:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High False-Positive Rates in Heuristic Systems:} Purely rule-based matching engines evaluate fields in isolation and cannot model complex non-linear interactions between order quantity, tiered pricing, and shipping surcharges. Furneaux and Wade \cite{furneaux} demonstrated that heuristic verification routines produce false-positive rates exceeding 35\% in enterprise settings, leading staff to habitually bypass automated alerts.
    \item \textbf{Opaque Scoring and Hallucination Risks:} Supervised deep neural networks often output abstract risk scores without human-verifiable arithmetic justifications. Furthermore, attempting to use ungrounded Large Language Models (LLMs) to inspect invoices introduces severe risks of numerical hallucination and non-deterministic calculations, which are unacceptable in regulatory financial audits \cite{ji_hallucination}.
    \item \textbf{Ingestion Fragility and Single-Points-of-Failure:} Real-world AP operations receive invoices in bulk CSV submissions. Existing systems frequently lack cryptographic deduplication, fail entirely when encountering a single syntax error in a batch, and crash when local or cloud database network drops occur.
\end{enumerate}

VIPS bridges these gaps by decoupling probabilistic risk triage from deterministic analytics, wrapping the entire workflow in an asynchronous batch ingestion pipeline with automatic local SQLite failover and a responsive web dashboard.

\section{Related Work}
% =========================================================

\subsection{Supervised Tabular Learning in Financial Auditing}

The application of supervised ensemble learning to financial and procurement auditing is well established. Breiman \cite{breiman} demonstrated that bagging de-correlated decision trees (Random Forests) exhibits superior generalization over tabular datasets containing non-linear feature interactions and skewed financial distributions. Pedregosa et al. \cite{pedregosa} and Fawcett \cite{fawcett} provide standard computational frameworks for scikit-learn model execution and ROC evaluation metrics.

\subsection{Grounded Analytics and Hallucination Mitigation}

Ji et al. \cite{ji_hallucination} surveyed hallucination in natural language generation, demonstrating that ungrounded generative systems readily produce plausible yet inaccurate figures. Lewis et al. \cite{lewis_rag} formulated Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to anchor conversational responses in retrieved evidence. VIPS operationalizes this principle by anchoring the analytics assistant entirely in 17 pre-defined, deterministic SQLAlchemy analytical routines rather than unconstrained language model generation.

\subsection{Asynchronous Microservices for ML Serving}

Tiwari and Kumar \cite{tiwari} proved that asynchronous ASGI Python microframeworks deliver significantly higher request throughput and lower latency than traditional WSGI frameworks, justifying the selection of FastAPI for low-latency inference serving. \section{Objectives and Scope}
% =========================================================

\subsection{Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dual-Model ML Serving:} Provide sub-millisecond inference for invoice risk classification and objective freight cost baseline estimation.
    \item \textbf{Catalog PO Resolution \& Sanity Verification:} Automatically match incoming invoices against the \texttt{purchases} catalog table to fetch authorized baseline quantities and dollars while enforcing strict numeric boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Asynchronous Batch Ingestion:} Ingest bulk CSV files via FastAPI \texttt{BackgroundTasks} with whole-file SHA-256 deduplication, 100-row chunking, and isolated row-level error tracking.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic SQL Analytics:} Implement 17 high-performance SQL analytical queries with in-memory regex intent classification and low-sample size warnings ($<5$ invoices).
    \item \textbf{Resilient Dual-Database Persistence:} Ensure uninterrupted audit operations via SQLAlchemy ORM with automatic zero-downtime failover from PostgreSQL to an embedded SQLite database.
    \item \textbf{Production-Ready Manifest Desk UI:} Provide a responsive, single-page web interface with real-time audit tables, multi-factor filtering, and batch job monitoring.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope}
VIPS is trained and evaluated on wholesale distribution invoice data denominated in USD. The system processes both manual single-entry submissions and high-volume CSV batch files. Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for scanned paper PDFs and multi-currency foreign exchange reconciliation are scoped as future extensions. VIPS is designed strictly as an automated decision-support system: it scores, estimates, and highlights anomalies, while ultimate payment approval or rejection authority remains with authorized finance reviewers.

\section{Proposed System Architecture}
% =========================================================

VIPS is structured into four cleanly decoupled operational layers behind an asynchronous ASGI server:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layer 1 -- Presentation Client \& API Gateway:} A responsive Vanilla HTML5/CSS/JavaScript single-page Manifest Desk served directly via FastAPI \texttt{StaticFiles}. Requests are strictly parsed and validated against Pydantic~v2 schemas across modular routers (\texttt{/api/invoice}, \texttt{/api/freight}, \texttt{/api/batches}, \texttt{/api/rag}).
    \item \textbf{Layer 2 -- Ingestion \& Batch Engine:} Handles file format validation, whole-file SHA-256 deduplication hashing, and streaming batch processing. Files with $\le 500$ rows are executed synchronously; larger batches are dispatched to FastAPI \texttt{BackgroundTasks}. The engine performs bulk PO pre-fetching, 100-row transactional database chunking, and captures malformed rows in an isolated \texttt{batch\_row\_errors} table.
    \item \textbf{Layer 3 -- Machine Learning Intelligence Core:} Evaluates a 300-tree \texttt{RandomForestClassifier} loaded as a singleton in memory via Joblib. Normalizes five features using a persisted \texttt{StandardScaler}, executes an OLS linear regressor for freight baselining, and outputs calibrated risk probabilities.
    \item \textbf{Layer 4 -- Resilient Persistence \& Analytics Layer:} Connects via SQLAlchemy~2.0 to primary PostgreSQL with automatic fallback to local SQLite (\texttt{vendor\_intelligence.db}). Houses the deterministic SQL analytics assistant executing 17 specialized analytical queries with in-memory intent classification.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Algorithmic Formulation}
% =========================================================

\subsection{Single Invoice Audit Pipeline}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:single_invoice} formalizes the end-to-end audit pipeline for an individual invoice transaction.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{VIPS Single Invoice Audit Pipeline}
\label{alg:single_invoice}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Invoice payload $I = (q_{\text{inv}}, d_{\text{inv}}, f_{\text{inv}})$, Vendor $V$, PO $P$
\ENSURE Verdict $y$, Probability $p$, Freight baseline $\hat{F}$, Record $R$
\STATE Validate $I$ against Pydantic~v2 schema (check $q>0, d>0, f\ge 0$)
\STATE $(q_{\text{po}}, d_{\text{po}}) \leftarrow \text{LookupPurchasesCatalog}(\text{db}, V, P)$
\STATE $\mathbf{x} \leftarrow [q_{\text{inv}}, d_{\text{inv}}, f_{\text{inv}}, q_{\text{po}}, d_{\text{po}}]$
\STATE $\mathbf{z} \leftarrow \text{StandardScaler.transform}(\mathbf{x})$
\STATE $y \leftarrow \text{RandomForestClassifier.predict}(\mathbf{z})$
\STATE $p \leftarrow \text{RandomForestClassifier.predict\_proba}(\mathbf{z})[0][1]$
\STATE $\hat{F}_{\text{raw}} \leftarrow \beta_1 \cdot d_{\text{inv}} + \beta_0$
\STATE $\hat{F} \leftarrow \max(0.0, \min(\hat{F}_{\text{raw}}, d_{\text{inv}}))$
\STATE $R \leftarrow \text{InsertInvoicePrediction}(\text{db}, V, P, I, y, p, \hat{F}, \text{source}=\text{`MANUAL'})$
\STATE $\text{db.commit()}; \text{db.refresh}(R)$
\RETURN $y, p, \hat{F}, R$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Asynchronous Batch Ingestion and Deduplication}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:batch_ingest} describes the batch processing pipeline with whole-file cryptographic deduplication and row error containment.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Asynchronous Batch Ingestion \& Fault Isolation}
\label{alg:batch_ingest}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Raw CSV bytes $B$, Filename $F$, Database session $\text{db}$
\ENSURE Batch job record $B_{\text{rec}}$ with UUID and execution metrics
\IF{$\neg F.\text{endswith}(\text{`.csv'}) \lor |B| == 0 \lor |B| > 10\,\text{MB}$}
    \STATE \textbf{reject} with HTTP 400 Bad Request
\ENDIF
\STATE $h \leftarrow \text{SHA256}(B)$ \COMMENT{Compute whole-file cryptographic hash}
\IF{$\exists \text{ batch} \in \text{upload\_batches} \mid \text{file\_hash} = h \land \text{status} \ne \text{`FAILED'}$}
    \STATE \textbf{raise} DuplicateBatchException(`Batch already processed.')
\ENDIF
\STATE $u \leftarrow \text{UUIDv4}()$
\STATE $B_{\text{rec}} \leftarrow \text{InsertUploadBatch}(\text{db}, u, F, h, \text{status}=\text{`PROCESSING'})$
\STATE $\text{df} \leftarrow \text{DecodeAndParseCSV}(B, \text{encodings}=[\text{utf-8-sig}, \text{utf-8}, \text{latin-1}])$
\STATE $B_{\text{rec}}.\text{row\_count} \leftarrow |\text{df}|; \text{db.commit}()$
\STATE $\text{pairs} \leftarrow \text{ExtractDistinctPairs}(\text{df}, \text{VendorNumber}, \text{PONumber})$
\STATE $\text{PO\_cache} \leftarrow \text{BulkLookupPurchases}(\text{db}, \text{pairs})$
\FORALL{$\text{chunk} \in \text{SplitIntoChunks}(\text{df}, \text{size}=100)$}
    \FORALL{$\text{row} \in \text{chunk}$}
        \TRY
            \STATE $\text{ValidateSanity}(\text{row})$ \COMMENT{Check $q>0, d>0, f\ge 0, d\le \$10\text{M}$}
            \STATE $(q_{\text{po}}, d_{\text{po}}) \leftarrow \text{PO\_cache.get}((\text{row}.v, \text{row}.p), (0, 0))$
            \STATE $\text{ScoreAndSaveInvoice}(\text{db}, \text{row}, q_{\text{po}}, d_{\text{po}}, \text{batch\_id}=B_{\text{rec}}.\text{id})$
            \STATE $B_{\text{rec}}.\text{success\_count} \leftarrow B_{\text{rec}}.\text{success\_count} + 1$
        \EXCEPT{ValidationError $\text{err}$}
            \STATE $\text{LogBatchRowError}(\text{db}, B_{\text{rec}}.\text{id}, \text{row}.n, \text{row.json}, \text{str}(\text{err}))$
            \STATE $B_{\text{rec}}.\text{error\_count} \leftarrow B_{\text{rec}}.\text{error\_count} + 1$
        \ENDTRY
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $\text{db.commit}()$ \COMMENT{100-row transactional commit}
\ENDFOR
\STATE $B_{\text{rec}}.\text{status} \leftarrow \text{`DONE'}; B_{\text{rec}}.\text{completed\_at} \leftarrow \text{now}(); \text{db.commit}()$
\RETURN $B_{\text{rec}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Deterministic SQL Analytics Routing}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:sql_analytics} details the in-memory intent routing and low-sample safeguard.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Deterministic SQL Analytics Assistant}
\label{alg:sql_analytics}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE User natural language query $Q$, Database session $\text{db}$
\ENSURE Formatted deterministic Markdown response $M$

\ENDIF

\RETURN $M$ \COMMENT{Instant execution ($<15$\,ms), zero token cost}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Mathematical Modeling}
% =========================================================

\subsection{Feature Standardization}
Input vectors are centered and scaled using parameters saved at training time:
\begin{equation}
z_j = \frac{x_j - \mu_j}{\sigma_j}, \quad j \in \{1, 2, \dots, 5\}
\end{equation}
where the five features are: billed invoice quantity ($q_{\text{inv}}$), billed invoice dollars ($d_{\text{inv}}$), freight surcharge ($f_{\text{inv}}$), authorized PO quantity ($q_{\text{po}}$), and authorized PO dollars ($d_{\text{po}}$).

\subsection{Random Forest Posterior Formulation}
The risk classifier consists of an ensemble of $B = 300$ bagged decision trees minimizing the Gini impurity criterion:
\begin{equation}
I_G(t) = 1 - \sum_{k=0}^{1} [p(k \mid t)]^2
\end{equation}
The ensemble posterior risk probability represents the mean vote across all constituent trees:
\begin{equation}
P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{Z}) = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^{B} p_b(y=1 \mid \mathbf{Z})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = 
\begin{cases} 
1 \text{ (FLAGGED)}, & \text{if } P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{Z}) \ge 0.50 \\ 
0 \text{ (CLEARED)}, & \text{if } P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{Z}) < 0.50 
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ordinary Least Squares Freight Regressor}
The baseline expected freight cost is modeled against invoice dollar amount $D$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{F}(D) = 0.00501517 \cdot D + 5.00802058
\end{equation}
Equation~(5) establishes an objective variable freight rate of approximately \$5.02 per \$1,000 in invoiced goods plus a fixed \$5.01 baseline handling fee. The prediction is clamped to physical bounds:
\begin{equation}
\hat{F}_{\text{clamped}} = \max(0.0, \min(\hat{F}(D), D))
\end{equation}
which prevents negative shipping estimates or freight charges exceeding merchandise value.

\subsection{Dual-Database Resilience Mechanism}
During application boot, VIPS executes a connection probe against PostgreSQL using SQLAlchemy with \texttt{pool\_pre\_ping=True}. If PostgreSQL is unreachable or credentials fail, the application catches the operational exception, logs a warning, and automatically instantiates a local SQLite database engine (\texttt{vendor\_intelligence.db}) with \texttt{check\_same\_thread=False}. Because table schemas are unified under declarative ORM models, the API gateway continues processing predictions, batch uploads, and audit queries without service downtime.

\section{Module Design}
% =========================================================

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Module 1: Invoice Risk Classification (\texttt{ml\_models.py}):} Scales features and evaluates the 300-tree Random Forest classifier, returning binary verdicts and risk probabilities.
    \item \textbf{Module 2: Freight Baseline Estimation (\texttt{ml\_models.py}):} Applies the fitted OLS regressor to provide an objective freight benchmark clamped to physical dollar bounds.
    \item \textbf{Module 3: Catalog PO Verification \& Input Sanity (\texttt{batch\_processor.py}, \texttt{services.py}):} Matches vendor and PO numbers against the \texttt{purchases} catalog table, validating numeric positivity, date syntax, and enforcing a \$10,000,000 safety ceiling.
    \item \textbf{Module 4: Asynchronous Batch Ingestion Engine (\texttt{batch\_processor.py}):} Manages CSV ingestion, whole-file SHA-256 deduplication hashing, UUID~v4 job tracking, 100-row chunked database commits, and row-level error isolation.
    \item \textbf{Module 5: Deterministic SQL Analytics Engine (\texttt{rag\_service.py}):} Resolves 17 structured analytical queries (flag rates, spend rankings, freight ratios, dollar mismatches, vendor comparisons) via in-memory regex intent routing and low-sample size guards ($<5$ invoices).
    \item \textbf{Module 6: Resilient Dual-Database Persistence (\texttt{database.py}, \texttt{models\_db.py}):} Coordinates SQLAlchemy ORM sessions across primary PostgreSQL with automatic zero-downtime failover to local SQLite.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Latency and Operational Benchmarking}
Local benchmarking on an Intel Core i7 / 16\,GB RAM workstation indicates that inference latency is under 2.0\,ms for the Random Forest classifier and under 0.5\,ms for the OLS freight regressor. End-to-end synchronous HTTP response times average 38\,ms. In-memory intent classification and deterministic SQL execution in the analytics engine complete in under 15\,ms with zero API token consumption. For bulk batch processing, pre-fetching PO tuples into an in-memory dictionary prior to chunked database insertion reduces roundtrips by over 95\%, enabling ingestion throughput of approximately 350--500 rows per second.

\section{Conclusion and Future Scope}
% =========================================================

This paper presented the Vendor Intelligence and Performance System (VIPS), an enterprise-ready invoice audit automation framework. The platform successfully realizes non-blocking asynchronous CSV batch ingestion, pre-inference cryptographic SHA-256 deduplication, row-level error containment, catalog purchase-order verification, dual-database persistence with automatic SQLite failover, and a 17-query deterministic SQL analytics assistant.

Future work will focus on integrating multimodal Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for scanned paper and PDF invoices, introducing multi-currency foreign exchange reconciliation, and implementing multi-tenant enterprise access controls.
